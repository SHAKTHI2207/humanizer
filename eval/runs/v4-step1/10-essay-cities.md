Few policies shape a city as quietly as parking minimums. They rarely make headlines, but they decide a lot about what gets built and where.

Parking minimums affect housing, affordability, and the kind of communities we end up with. Requiring developers to include parking raises the cost of new housing, which prices out many of the residents cities say they want to serve.

Consider Minneapolis. In 2021, the city eliminated parking minimums citywide. I think that was the right call, and it shows what a city can do when it puts people ahead of pavement.

I'm not saying parking never matters. Some neighborhoods do have real shortages. But the question is who should decide how much parking a building needs, the city or the people building and using it. I'd leave that to them.
