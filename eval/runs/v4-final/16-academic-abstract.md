Sleep supports memory consolidation, but its effect on academic performance has received little study. We examined the relationship between sleep duration and memory performance in 84 undergraduates, using wrist actigraphy to measure sleep objectively over 14 nights. Sleep duration correlated positively with next-day word-list recall (r = 0.31), and the correlation was significant. We found no effect on reaction time. These results suggest that adequate sleep may matter for students' cognitive functioning, although a correlational design cannot show that improving sleep would raise achievement. The study adds to research on sleep and cognition and may inform policy in higher education.
