Few urban planning policies have done as much, with as little attention, as parking minimums. These rules shape a large part of how our cities look and work.

They seem like a rule about cars, but they are also about housing, what it costs, and what kind of communities we build. Requiring developers to include parking raises the cost of new housing, and that effectively prices out the residents cities say they want to serve.

Consider Minneapolis. In 2021, the city eliminated parking minimums citywide. It was a bold move toward a more equitable city, and it shows what's possible when a city puts people ahead of pavement.

Parking still matters, and some neighborhoods do have real shortages. The question is who should decide how much.
