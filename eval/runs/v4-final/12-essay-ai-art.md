AI-generated art is one of the biggest changes to creative work in my lifetime. I'm a working illustrator, and from where I sit it brings real promise and real danger at once.

In 2024 I lost two clients to image generators. It was a professional setback, but it also hit me personally, and it made me ask what human creativity is worth now.

The story is more complicated than that, though. I still use AI tools to gather reference material, and they make parts of my workflow better. So I resist these tools and use them at the same time.

The central question for me is consent. Nobody has resolved whether the artists whose work trained these models agreed to it, and that problem hangs over the industry's future.

Artists, technologists, and policymakers will have to work this out together, in a way that is fair to the people whose creativity these systems are built on and still leaves room for new tools.
