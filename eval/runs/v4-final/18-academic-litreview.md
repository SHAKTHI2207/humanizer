Research on remote work has grown quickly in recent years as workplaces have changed. Numerous studies have shown that remote work improves employee well-being (Smith & Zhang, 2021). In a randomized experiment at a Chinese travel agency, Bloom et al. (2015) found a 13% increase in performance among employees working from home. Gibbs et al. (2023), by contrast, documented a decline in productivity at an Indian IT services company after the move to working from home. Together these studies suggest that the outcome depends on context, and more work is needed on the conditions under which remote work helps or hinders productivity. On current evidence, remote work is neither uniformly beneficial nor uniformly harmful.
