Sleep contributes to memory consolidation, but its effect on academic performance has received less study. This study examined how sleep duration relates to memory performance in 84 undergraduate students, using wrist actigraphy to measure sleep objectively across 14 nights. Students who slept longer recalled more items on a word list the next day, a significant positive correlation (r = 0.31). Sleep duration had no effect on reaction time. The results suggest that improving sleep could help students perform better, and they add to the literature on sleep and cognition in ways that may inform evidence-based policy in higher education.
