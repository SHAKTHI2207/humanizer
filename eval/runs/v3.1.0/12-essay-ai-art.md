AI-generated art is one of the biggest shifts in illustration in years, and as a working illustrator I'm in the middle of it, with reasons for hope and for worry.

In 2024, I lost two clients to image generators. It was a professional setback, and it was personal too: it made me question what human creativity is worth now.

It's not all one-sided. I still use AI tools for reference gathering, and they speed up my workflow. So I resist them and use them at the same time.

The core issue is consent. The training-data consent problem is still unresolved, and it hangs over the industry's future.

Artists, technologists, and policymakers need to work together on a path that honors creativity and is fair, while still leaving room for innovation. Until they do, the technology's potential stays largely unrealized.
