Few urban planning policies have been as consequential, or as little noticed, as parking minimums. These rules shape how our cities are built.

Parking minimums affect housing, affordability, and the kind of communities we build. Requiring developers to include parking raises the cost of new housing, which prices out the residents cities say they want to serve.

Consider Minneapolis. In 2021 the city eliminated parking minimums citywide. I think that was the right call, and it showed that a city can put people ahead of pavement.

Parking still matters, and some neighborhoods do face genuine shortages. But the open question is who should decide how much parking a project gets.

Cities should drop the minimums and stop mandating a fixed amount.
