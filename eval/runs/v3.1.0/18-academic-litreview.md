Research on remote work has grown quickly in recent years as workplaces have changed. Numerous studies have found that remote work improves employee well-being (Smith & Zhang, 2021). In a seminal randomized experiment at a Chinese travel agency, Bloom et al. (2015) found that employees working from home performed 13% better. Gibbs et al. (2023), by contrast, found that productivity fell at an Indian IT services company after its staff moved to working from home. These results suggest that the effect of remote work depends on context, and more research is needed on the conditions under which it helps or hurts productivity.
