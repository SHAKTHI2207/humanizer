Few policies have had as much quiet influence on cities as parking minimums. Most people never notice them, yet they shape what gets built and where.

Parking minimums affect housing, affordability, and what kind of communities we end up with. When developers are required to include parking, new housing costs more to build, and that prices out the residents cities say they want to serve.

Take Minneapolis. In 2021 the city eliminated parking minimums citywide. I think that was a bold decision, and it showed that a city can put people ahead of pavement.

Parking still matters, and some neighborhoods do face real shortages. But the useful question is who should decide how much parking gets built.

I think that should be the people building and using the places, not a blanket rule. Cities should be willing to change the rules, because how we house people depends on it.
